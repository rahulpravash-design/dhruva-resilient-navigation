# SpeedNet: what the synthetic results do and do not show

SpeedNet estimates forward speed (and its own uncertainty) from 1.5 s of levelled IMU data. Its value in the real
world comes from vibration that grows with speed (tyre/road/wheel-rotation content in the accelerometer).

**We have no real data yet.** The simulator's IMU had no such vibration, so an *assumed* model was added
(`ImuParams.vib_gain` in `engine_py/dhruva/sim/imu.py`): accel vibration in the vehicle frame with standard deviation
`vib_gain * speed`, white plus a tone at the wheel-rotation frequency (`speed / (2*pi*0.31 m)`). `vib_gain` is drawn per
drive from 0.02-0.05 m/s^2 per m/s.

Consequences, stated plainly:
- The network is trained and evaluated on data generated by the same assumed mechanism. A held-out improvement on SYNTHETIC
  data shows the plumbing works (train -> TFLite -> EKF pseudo-measurement -> smaller drift). It is **not** evidence that
  SpeedNet works on a real phone in a real car.
- Real vibration differs (mounts, suspension, road surface, engine, other passengers); expect retraining and possibly
  a different window length or architecture on GSDC / own-drive data.
- If SpeedNet does not reduce held-out drift on real data, the honest fallback is to report that and keep the NHC-only
  pipeline as the headline result (master spec section 7.3).
- Alignment error is emulated in training windows with ~1 deg tilt and ~2 deg yaw perturbations.
- Input must be resampled to 100 Hz; the live app has to do that before feeding the engine.
